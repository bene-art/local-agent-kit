# Photosynthesis — short overview

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and certain bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy.

## The basic reaction

Carbon dioxide and water, in the presence of light, produce glucose and oxygen:

    6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light → C6H12O6 + 6 O2

The reaction is catalyzed by chlorophyll, the green pigment in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs light most strongly in the blue and red wavelengths and reflects green, which is why plants appear green.

## Two stages

1. **Light-dependent reactions** occur in the thylakoid membranes. They split water molecules, releasing oxygen as a byproduct, and store energy in ATP and NADPH.

2. **Light-independent reactions** (the Calvin cycle) occur in the stroma of the chloroplast. They use the ATP and NADPH from the first stage to fix carbon dioxide into glucose.

## Why it matters

Photosynthesis is the foundation of most food chains on Earth. It also produces the oxygen in the atmosphere — virtually all of the oxygen we breathe comes from photosynthesis, either by current plants or by ancient cyanobacteria.
